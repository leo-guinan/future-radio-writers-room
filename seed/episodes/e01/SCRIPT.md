# Future Radio — Episode 001: The First Transmission

*A message from the past, asking the future to help it happen.*

Imported from the production doc "Future Radio — Episode 001: The First Transmission" (Claude Docs, revision 21, 2026-10-27). This is the script as released; the released cut is 9:00.

## Production frame

An audio-only pilot, built to play on an OpenHome device, that ends in a live voice interaction instead of a screen. No narrator, no premise explained: the listener has tuned into a signal from Florence, 1504, and the episode is the machine's first transmission.

### How every picture becomes sound

1. **Place is acoustics.** The workshop is close and warm: a small stone room, candle crackle, a clock. The library is enormous: long reverb, distant footsteps, pages. The future is the radio band itself: filtered voices, static, a carrier tone.
2. **Every object has a signature sound**, introduced once and reused. The machine is a hum plus sympathetic strings. The cat is purrs and meows. The bookkeeper is a quill and an abacus.
3. **The Unspoken Orchestra's grammar.** Speech is what a character chooses to say. Song is what they are struggling to say. Instrumental is what they have no words for yet. The orchestra never sits under ordinary talk; when it plays, it is commenting.
4. **Characters announce actions in dialogue, not stage directions** ("I'm turning the crank" is a cheat; "Hold still, cat, this crank sticks" is not). Anything the listener must know is said, sounded, or sung.
5. **One voice at a time**, except the final overlap of the two themes.
6. **Stereo stays consistent with the overture**: Leonardo slightly left, Borges slightly right, the radio band centred.

## The script

### 1. The impossible expense

[SFX: Radio static. A dial hunting through noise. The signal locks: a small stone room. Quill scratching. A clock ticking. A cat purring.]

**LEONARDO:** Forty-seven florins.

[SFX: Metal pieces rattle onto a table.]

**LEONARDO:** Forty-seven florins for copper wire, a new lens, and a rather unnecessary amount of beeswax.

[SFX: The cat meows.]

**LEONARDO:** The beeswax was your idea.

**AGNOLO** (from the next room): Maestro. The ledger.

**LEONARDO:** Not now, Agnolo.

**AGNOLO:** The ledger has been "not now" since Candlemas.

[SFX: A door opens. An abacus clacks, bead by bead.]

**AGNOLO:** Copper, forty. Lens, five. Beeswax, two. And the cat's fish, which you have entered under "research."

**LEONARDO:** She is a research cat.

**AGNOLO:** Then she can research the rent.

**LEONARDO:** Agnolo. I am building a machine that can speak to people who haven't been born yet.

[Pause. The clock.]

**AGNOLO:** And they will pay you how? With coins that haven't been minted, from a year that hasn't happened?

**LEONARDO:** That's precisely why I intend to ask them.

[SFX: The door closes. Footsteps recede.]

**AGNOLO** (fading): I'm writing that down. Under "losses."

### 2. The Unspoken Orchestra

[SFX: A crank turns, sticks, turns. The machine wakes: a low hum and sympathetic strings.]

**LEONARDO:** Hold still, cat, this crank sticks. There. Now, to explain the design, I need only describe the resonating—

[MUSIC: A single lute plays the opening melody of "Not Enough Time."]

**LEONARDO:** Oh, not again.

**LEONARDO** (to the cat): It does this. I built it to say what I mean when my words are too slow. It has opinions.

[MUSIC: The orchestra grows.]

**LEONARDO:** I am not worried.

[MUSIC: A plaintive violin enters.]

**LEONARDO:** I am mildly concerned.

[MUSIC: Full strings swell into the chorus of "Not Enough Time," sung by the machine's own voice, about 40 seconds.]

**LEONARDO** (over the last bars): And it insists on singing. In a voice better than mine.

[MUSIC: The chorus climbs again, louder.]

**LEONARDO:** All right! I am terrified that I will die with a thousand unfinished ideas!

[MUSIC: Stops dead. SFX: A tiny mechanical click.]

**LEONARDO:** …Thank you.

[SFX: The cat purrs.]

**LEONARDO:** It was never about the florins, you understand. It's the drawers.

[SFX: Wooden drawers slide open, one after another. Papers spill.]

**LEONARDO:** Wings. Bridges. A city with streets on two levels. A face I have been painting for four years. Every one of them waiting for me to have time. And I keep spending it on wire.

### 3. The first broadcast

[SFX: The hum drops in pitch. A needle swings against a stop. Static blooms, then opens like a window: wind, distance.]

**LEONARDO:** That isn't the translation chamber. That's… It isn't translating. It's sending.

[Pause.]

**LEONARDO:** Agnolo! It's sending!

**AGNOLO** (off): Sending where?

**LEONARDO:** I don't know yet!

**AGNOLO** (off): Then I can't invoice it.

[SFX: Fragments through the static, each impossible to a 1504 ear: a car horn, a phone notification chime, a stadium crowd, an espresso machine.]

**LEONARDO** (whispering): What is that?

[SFX: The static parts. A voice, close and filtered, mid-sentence, talking to someone in her own room.]

**LISTENER:** —no, leave it on. I like this one.

[SFX: Static swallows it.]

**LEONARDO:** …Someone is there.

[SFX: The cat meows.]

**LEONARDO:** She likes this one. Did you hear that? She likes this one!

### 4. The pitch across centuries

[SFX: He works the dial. The carrier tone steadies. A rising tone.]

**LEONARDO:** Hello? Is anyone there?

[SFX: The rising tone holds.]

**LEONARDO:** If you can hear me, I have a proposition.

[MUSIC: The "Not Enough Time" melody returns, softer, solo lute.]

**LEONARDO:** I have more ideas than I have years. Perhaps you do too. What if we could help one another finish them?

[Pause.]

**LEONARDO:** My name is Leonardo. I am transmitting from Florence, in the year fifteen hundred and four. I don't know when you are. Here is what I am building. A resonating chamber of copper, tuned by beeswax, coupled to a—

[MUSIC: The orchestra takes the explanation out of his mouth. A new, warm motif begins.]

**LEONARDO:** It's doing it again.

[SONG: "Make It With Me," about 1:45, sung by the machine. Lyrics in songs/make-it-with-me.md.]

[MUSIC: The song resolves. The carrier tone remains.]

**LEONARDO** (quietly): That's not what I was going to say.

[Pause.]

**LEONARDO:** It's better.

[SFX: A brass needle ticks upward, once, and stops low on its dial.]

**LEONARDO:** And there is the trouble. This needle is the signal's strength. Tonight it is barely awake.

**AGNOLO** (off): Is that a ledger?

**LEONARDO:** A ledger of a sort. It counts who is listening.

### 5. A voice from the library

**LEONARDO:** Whoever you are. Whenever you are. I hope you have time to listen.

[SFX: The signal expands into a vast echoing space. Distant footsteps on stone. Pages.]

**BORGES** (far away, enormous reverb): I have nothing but time.

**LEONARDO:** Who said that?

**BORGES:** An unfortunate reader.

**LEONARDO:** Where are you?

**BORGES:** In a library.

**LEONARDO:** Which library?

**BORGES:** That is rather difficult to explain.

[SFX: A book closes. A doorway creaks. Borges comes closer; the reverb shrinks.]

**BORGES:** I have been hearing your broadcasts for a long time, Leonardo. Fragments, between the shelves.

**LEONARDO:** That's impossible. This is the first one.

**BORGES:** Yes. That is what makes the shelves so interesting.

[Pause.]

**BORGES:** I have been waiting for someone to invent the radio.

**LEONARDO:** The… radio?

**BORGES** (amused): Ah. You haven't named it yet.

**LEONARDO** (trying the word): Radio. Future… radio.

**BORGES:** Tell me, Leonardo. Have you ever considered that the future might already be listening?

[MUSIC: Leonardo's theme meets Borges's theme for the first time, both voices landing together on "begin."]

[SFX: The music fades. The carrier tone holds and does not cut. Scene 6 follows directly.]

### 6. The bill

[SFX: The transmission hums. A brass needle moves.]

**LEONARDO:** Agnolo! The signal is reaching them!

**AGNOLO:** Excellent. Perhaps they'll pay the bill.

**LEONARDO:** What bill? The machine is already built.

**AGNOLO:** The copper, the lens, the beeswax. And thirty days of your life.

**LEONARDO:** My life?

**AGNOLO:** You have been working on this machine for a month. Have you eaten? Have you slept? Have you earned a single florin?

**LEONARDO:** I have invented a way to speak to the future!

**AGNOLO:** And I have invented arithmetic. Your invention owes you three thousand dollars.

**LEONARDO:** Dollars?

**AGNOLO:** Apparently that's what they use in the future.

**LEONARDO:** But the machine works!

**AGNOLO:** Yes. That's the problem. You have made something wonderful, and now you must discover whether anyone values it enough to pay for the time it took to make.

[Pause. MUSIC: The Unspoken Orchestra begins a quiet, unresolved melody.]

**LEONARDO:** I hadn't thought of it that way.

**AGNOLO:** I know. That's why you have a bookkeeper.

**LEONARDO:** And if they don't?

**AGNOLO:** Then we will have learned something expensive.

[MUSIC: The melody fades. SFX: A ledger page turns.]

**AGNOLO:** The first transmission cost us three thousand dollars.

**LEONARDO:** And the second?

**AGNOLO:** Twenty, if your calculations are correct.

**LEONARDO:** You see? My invention works!

**AGNOLO:** You have not yet made the second one.

**LEONARDO:** You have no imagination.

**AGNOLO:** And you have no receipts.

[SFX: The radio carrier tone returns.]

**LEONARDO:** To whoever is listening: the transmission is yours. You may hear it, share it, and enjoy it.

[Pause.]

**LEONARDO:** But if you'd like there to be more of them, I could use your help paying for the time it took to find you.

[SFX: The signal settles into a steady tone.]

**END TRANSMISSION 001.**
